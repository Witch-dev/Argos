# Pass: quieter

Reduce visual intensity without losing character. Quiet design is harder than bold: it needs precision. Generic quiet (grey text on white, one weight, a grid of identical cards) is a failure, not restraint.

On Winged Words' Operate and Read screens (DESIGN.md "Density by kind of screen") quieter means **less noise**: fewer accents, flatter surfaces, less colour, less motion, so the tool disappears into the reading. On the landing page it means more air and less drama, with the point of view intact.

## Find the sources of intensity
Saturation, contrast extremes, too many heavy elements competing, motion, decoration, everything at the same large scale. Also ask: what is the screen for, and what already works (keep it)?

## How
- **Colour:** fewer colours used more deliberately; neutrals do most of the work and the green owns its role (actions, selection), never decoration. Never grey text on a coloured surface: use a darker shade of that colour.
- **Weight:** step weights down (700 → 600, 600 → 500); hierarchy through weight, size and space instead of colour.
- **Simplify:** remove gradients, extra shadows, patterns and borders that do no job; flatten layers; no card inside a card.
- **Motion:** shorter distances (10–20px, not 40), keep only DESIGN.md's feedback/state/focal motion.
- **Composition:** smaller jumps between sizes, rogue elements back onto the spacing scale, even rhythm.

## Avoid
Everything the same size, removing all colour, removing personality, weakening affordances (buttons still look like buttons), making everything small and light (keep anchors).

## Check
Still functional, still distinctive, easier to read for a long time, the point of view survived. Then `polish`.
